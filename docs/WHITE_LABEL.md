# White label

Companies offer moving and delivery under their own brand, with FlyttGo's drivers doing the moves (owner's decision, 2026-10-29; [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) #22–25). Step 6 of the corporate plan. Migration `supabase/migrations/00009_white_label.sql`, rollback `supabase/rollback/00009_down.sql`.

**Not applied or deployed.** It follows the same procedure as the rest: [`SECURITY_P0.md`](SECURITY_P0.md) §5 and [`COPY_OF_PRODUCTION_TEST_PLAN.md`](COPY_OF_PRODUCTION_TEST_PLAN.md).

## The two seller models

| | Model A: FlyttGo sells (the default) | Model B: the company sells |
|---|---|---|
| Who the customer buys from | FlyttGo AS, on the company's page | The company |
| Price | FlyttGo's price | FlyttGo's price plus the company's markup (never below what FlyttGo takes) |
| Payment | Card or Vipps, to FlyttGo | Card, into the company's own Stripe account (Stripe Connect, Standard account, direct charge) |
| Receipt, terms, consumer rights | FlyttGo | The company |
| Customer service, refunds, complaints | FlyttGo | The company first; FlyttGo carries out the moves |
| Customers' personal data | FlyttGo is the controller; the company sees numbers only | The company is the controller; FlyttGo processes it under the [data processing agreement](../src/pages/WhiteLabelDpaPage.tsx) |
| What FlyttGo earns | Its own sale, plus the plan's monthly fee | Its price for the move and a platform fee per booking (taken from the payment), plus the plan's fees |
| Needs | An active company and a display name | Also: MVA registration, customer service email and phone, its terms and privacy notice, the agreement accepted by an owner, a Stripe account that can take payments, a verified domain |

Model A asks more of FlyttGo (payment, receipts, support, refunds, complaints), so it is the simple choice for the company and the default. Model B suits larger companies with their own customer service and accounting.

## Plans and prices

The price list is the table `white_label_plans`, shown at `/business/white-label` and in the company dashboard. **Shipped prices are proposals** (all excluding MVA) until an administrator confirms them in Admin → White label → Plans and prices; until then every page says so. An administrator can also accept a plan's prices provisionally as **pilot pricing** (`admin_set_white_label_pilot_price`, with a note shown beside them; 00022); the owner did so for Restaurant, Hotel and Online shop on 2026-10-29 ([`SERVICE_PLATFORM.md`](SERVICE_PLATFORM.md)). A changed price becomes a proposal again. Add-on modules on a plan are described in [`SERVICE_PLATFORM.md`](SERVICE_PLATFORM.md) → Plans and prices.

| Plan | Model | Set-up | Monthly | Per booking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branded booking page (default) | A | 2 900 | 1 490 | none |
| Branded booking on your domain | A | 4 900 | 2 490 | none |
| Own seller | B | 14 900 | 4 990 | platform fee 4 % of FlyttGo's price excl. MVA, at least 49 NOK (+ MVA) |

**Billing** goes on FlyttGo's own invoices (00008), with the company's payment terms. The invoice run (`invoice-run`, daily) calls `create_white_label_invoice` for each company on a plan:
- the set-up fee, once per plan;
- the monthly fee in advance, each month from approval, with the first month counted from the start day, and nothing after the month a plan ends (suspended plans are still billed);
- for Model B, what FlyttGo kept from each booking finished or cancelled before this month (its price and the fee, or their share after a refund). That part is already collected through Stripe, so `settle_white_label_invoice` records it as paid (`platform_fee`) when the invoice is issued. The company owes only the plan fees.

Price changes apply to charges made after the change; customers' contracts decide notice.

## How a company gets a page

1. **Choose a plan** (owner or admin): Corporate dashboard → White label. Without a choice it is the default plan. A company already on a plan can request another; the old plan stays until FlyttGo approves the change.
2. **Brand:** display name, tagline, colours. The logo is the company's logo from Invoices. For Model B, also customer service contacts, terms and privacy links, and the markup, with a worked example of the split.
3. **Website** (optional): a template from the library (below) and the town it names.
4. **Domain** (plans with a domain): the company gives one, adds a TXT record `_flyttgo.<domain>` = `flyttgo-verify=<token>` and a CNAME to the hosting target, then presses **Check DNS** (`white-label-domain`, DNS over HTTPS). **FlyttGo then adds the domain to the Vercel project** (manual, see the checklist below).
5. **Model B:** an owner accepts the data processing agreement (version recorded) and connects Stripe (`stripe-connect`: creates a Standard account and returns Stripe's onboarding link; `account.updated` keeps the "can take payments" flag current).
6. **FlyttGo approves** in Admin → White label. The database refuses approval while anything is missing (`white_label_missing`). Administrators can also decline (with a reason), suspend, end and reactivate.

The page is at `flyttgo.no/b/<slug>`, at `<slug>.flyttgo.no` (once the wildcard is set up), and at the company's verified domain. It wears the company's header and footer; the footer always names the seller (FlyttGo or the company) with its organisation number.

## Bookings on a company's page

- The booking flow and payment page are FlyttGo's own (no second booking engine). The page's company goes on the booking (`white_label_org`); **the database decides the seller model from the active plan**, never the browser (`guard_booking_white_label`).
- Model B: `booking-charge` computes the split (`_shared/white-label.ts`, the same function the browser uses to show the price) and writes `customer_price`, `platform_fee` and `connected_account_id` (server only). `create-checkout-session` creates the Checkout session **on the company's account** with `application_fee_amount` = FlyttGo's price + fee. Vipps and invoice are refused for these bookings (Vipps would need the company's own Vipps agreement; the invoice is FlyttGo's).
- `stripe-webhook` accepts events from the Connect endpoint and marks a booking paid only when the event's account is the one the booking was charged on.
- Refunds of Model B bookings go through the company's account with `refund_application_fee`, so FlyttGo gives back its share in proportion. The cancellation policy is FlyttGo's (the company's terms must not promise less).
- Drivers, dispatch, escrow and payouts are unchanged: the escrow holds FlyttGo's price.
- Who sees the bookings: Model B, the selling company's members (`bookings_white_label_select`); Model A, nobody at the company (they see `white_label_stats`, monthly numbers only).
- On a plan outside transport the page books the company's own module instead (`bookingFor()` in `_shared/white-label.ts`, read by `white-label-site`): jobs, appointments, restaurant tables or hotel rooms ([`SERVICE_PLATFORM.md`](SERVICE_PLATFORM.md)), and a plan with the Shop module also shows the company's shop at `/b/<slug>/shop` (the page's only offer when it has no booking module); its seller notice then names the company as the seller of everything offered on the page and FlyttGo as the booking and payment platform.

## Website templates (from ArchitectPro)

Architect-AI's `site_masters` (210 generated five-page websites, read 2026-10-29) are brought in as a template library. **None has been imported into FlyttGo yet**: the import writes to FlyttGo's database and storage and needs the owner's approval, and production does not have migration 00009 yet.

- **What is taken:** every template except the 11 health templates (clinics, dentists, therapists: patient data is outside FlyttGo). That is **199 templates in 10 catalogue categories** (`TEMPLATE_CATEGORIES` in `supabase/functions/_shared/site-template.ts`): salons, wellness and bookings 37; food, hospitality and events 36; moving and transport 33; trades, home and car services 22; professional services 15; education, nonprofit and community 14; property, industry and agriculture 13; software and technology 12; shops 9; gaming and entertainment 8. A template whose industries match no category goes to "Other businesses"; none is left out silently.
- **Keeping the design:** the templates style themselves with their own `<style>` (no outside stylesheets or fonts) and use `{{ASSET:key}}` images, which are kept. 106 of them also have a small script that fades sections in (`.reveal` and others: in 82 templates whole sections start invisible), draws star ratings and counts numbers up. Pages are served without scripts, so before cleaning the importer opens each page in a headless browser with reduced motion and no network (`scripts/template-snapshot.ts`; all 106 scripts then show everything in its final state) and keeps the page as it ends up. It then shows the cleaned page with scripts off and flags it **hidden** if it shows less than 90 % of the text. Quote forms become a card in the form's own style with a "Get a price and book" button. The serve step also shows `.reveal` content as a safety net.
- **Import:** `scripts/import-site-templates.ts` reads Architect-AI (service key) or an export file, settles each page as above, draws the thumbnail (above; written to `<out>/thumbnails/<id>.jpg`), prepares it (`prepareTemplate`: strips scripts, event handlers, `javascript:` links, frames, forms, external styles and images and refreshes; turns the sample company's name, also where the page writes `&` as `&amp;` and in image descriptions, its phone and email into placeholders; points quote links at the booking page; lists prices, reviews and claims to check), and writes a review folder with `report.md` (by category). Needs Playwright (`npm i -D playwright && npx playwright install chromium`). Behind an HTTPS proxy, Node's own `fetch` ignores `HTTPS_PROXY`: run it with `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1` (Node 22.21 or later). With `--to-flyttgo` it copies the images into the `site-template-assets` bucket and stores each template through `import_site_template` (server only). **Running it against FlyttGo needs the owner's approval.**
- **Catalogue:** companies choose in Corporate → White label → Website (`src/components/whiteLabel/TemplateCatalogue.tsx`). The card shows the **current choice** (its picture, name, "Best for …", style, pages and features, **Preview all pages** and **Change**; "No template" when none is chosen; a chosen template that is no longer offered is marked) and a prominent **Browse all N websites** button (N: the approved templates). There is no small grid in the card.
- **Gallery** (`TemplateGallery.tsx`, rules in `gallery.ts`): a full-screen dialog in the style of theme galleries. Its header has the title, the number of websites, **search** (name, style, industries, features and category; every word must match), **sort** (Recommended: the catalogue's own order, today by name; or Name A–Z; no popularity is recorded, so none is shown) and Close. A sidebar on wider screens (from 1024 px) lists the **categories** with counts and the filters **What it has** (prices, menu, gallery, testimonials, team, FAQ, contact; every ticked one must be there; "services" is left out as nearly every template has it) and **Style** (a list of the templates' styles, most common first); on phones and small tablets the categories are a scrolling row of chips and the other filters open under a **Filters** button. Each count says what choosing it would show (counted with the other choices applied). The grid has 1 column on phones, 2 from 640 px, 3 from 1024 px, 4 from 1280 px and 5 from 1600 px. Above it: "Showing 24 of 199", the active filters (each removable) and Clear all. Cards come in batches of 24: the next batch loads as the "Show more" button nears the screen, and the button stays for keyboards and browsers without that. "No template" is the first card while nothing narrows the gallery; nothing matching shows "No website matches" with a way back to all. Choosing a website closes the gallery and shows it in the card; it is saved with **Save website** as before. Only owners and admins can choose; everyone in the company can look.
  - **Keyboard and screen readers:** the gallery and the preview are modal dialogs with a heading; focus moves into them (the gallery to its search), Tab stays inside, Escape closes the top one only (the preview above the gallery), the page behind does not scroll, and focus goes back to the button that opened them (`useDialog.ts`). A card's Preview and Choose are named by the template ("Preview Verve Hair Salon").
- **Preview** (`TemplatePreview.tsx`): every page in the company's own name, colours and contact details (sandboxed, without scripts, as published), labelled "Preview: your name and colours, sample content": at desktop width, as a tablet (768 px) or as a phone (390 px), page by page (page tabs, previous and next), with "Choose this website". Opened from the gallery it also steps to the previous or next website of the gallery's results ("3 of 10"); the gallery keeps its search, filters and order behind it. The list loads without pages; a template's pages are loaded when it is opened.
- **Cards** (migration 00021): each gallery card shows the template's **thumbnail**, a picture of its cleaned home page as the template's own demo (its own name and colours, as theme galleries show a demo site; the company's name and colours come in the preview), drawn by the importer in a desktop window of 1440 × 900 (what a visitor sees first), saved as a 720 × 450 JPEG and stored beside its images as the asset `thumbnail` (`site-template-assets/<id>/thumbnail.jpg`). Cards show it lazily (`loading="lazy"`), all in the same 16 : 10 shape (a desktop window), with a slight zoom on hover or focus that is switched off under reduced motion. **Preview** and **Choose** sit over the picture on hover and keyboard focus and are always shown on touch screens; the chosen template has a "✓ Chosen" mark and a ring. A template without a thumbnail shows a **plain card** with its name and style (never its hero photo, which in the source library is mostly the same stock photo, and never a live page: the grid draws no frames for it). The live miniature (`TemplateThumb.tsx`: the home page drawn in the company's name and colours, scaled down) is shown for the company's current choice in the Website card when that template has no up-to-date thumbnail, and in the grid only for a card whose thumbnail is stale (00024: as the template's own demo with the library's photos; [Image library](#image-library) → Thumbnails). Under the picture: the name, "Best for …" (its industries in words, `bestFor()`) and one line with the style, its number of pages (`page_count`, computed from the pages) and its **features** (services, prices, menu, gallery, testimonials, team, FAQ, contact; found by the importer in the pages' paths, headings and section names, `templateFeatures()`, stored in `features`).
  - **Tried with the whole library:** the gallery was drawn in a local harness with the card data of all 210 `site_masters` (id, name, industry, style and section names, read 2026-10-29): 199 after the 11 health templates, 4 with real thumbnails and the rest as plain cards. The features there were found from the section names only (the importer also reads headings, so it finds at least as many: a section named only "team" is not counted, a heading such as "Meet the team" is).
- **The company's brand in a template** (`prepareTemplate` at import, `renderTemplatePage` when served): the sample company's full name is replaced, and so are the **short forms** its logo uses (`sampleShortNames()`: the first word(s) of the name where a brand or logo element shows only them, e.g. "Verve" for "Verve Hair Salon"); a short form that is an everyday word, or that the template also writes in lower case, is replaced only in the logo, never in the text ("Fast" of "Fast Man & Van"). Names are also replaced in the title, image descriptions, labels and the page's description for search engines. Each brand or logo element showing the name becomes a **brand slot** (`fg-brand-slot`): the template's own mark, then the company's **logo** when it has one (Invoices → logo; it then takes the mark's place) and its name. A long name is set smaller with less letter spacing and wraps inside the slot instead of pushing the header apart or being clipped (names over 18 and over 40 characters); words too long for the screen break rather than overflow it. The company's colours are given as the template's own variables (`--c-primary`, `--c-accent`) and as `--fg-primary`, `--fg-accent`, `--fg-on-primary`, `--fg-on-accent` (readable text on them). Every company value is escaped and filled in one pass, so nothing the company writes is read as a placeholder; a logo must be an https link on the asset store's host.
- **Contrast:** Corporate → White label → Brand warns when white text on the main or accent colour (as most templates' buttons have it), which is also the colour as text on white, is below WCAG AA (4.5 : 1), and suggests the nearest darker shade that reads (`readableShade()`). The company's colours change only if it presses "Use" and saves; FlyttGo never changes them silently.
- **Quality gate** (`supabase/functions/_shared/template-quality.ts`, migration 00021): the importer checks every page automatically and stores the results with the template (`site_templates.checks`): **unsafe HTML** (the sanitiser's rules), **renders** (draws, with text), **overflow** (nothing wider than the screen at 320, 375 and 390 px, drawn with a long company name; the outermost elements that stick out are named), **broken images** (every `{{ASSET:key}}` has an image and it loads; when no image loads at all the importer could not reach the asset store and the check is "not run"), **image alt texts** (`alt=""` counts as decoration), **booking links** (every link that reads as booking, "Book now", "Reserve", "Get a free quote", "Request an appointment"…, leads to the booking page; the importer rewrites them, the check confirms it) and **sample content** (the content flags for the sample name and its short forms, a US address, town and state, phone, email, price or figure such as "18,000+" or "98%"; reviews and claims stay for the reviewer to judge). `report.md` lists the failing checks per template. **Approval is refused while a check fails or is out of date** unless the administrator gives a reason; the database records who, when, why and which checks in `check_overrides` (`admin_review_site_template(…, p_override_reason)`). Unsafe HTML and prices are never overridden. A template imported without checks counts as not checked. When an administrator saves a page, its results become out of date; the admin panel at once records the checks that need no browser and the page's content flags again from the saved page (`admin_record_site_template_page_checks`); the browser's (renders, overflow) wait for `node scripts/import-site-templates.ts --recheck [--ids …]` (writes only checks and flags; needs the owner's approval like `--to-flyttgo`) or a reason. Admin → White label → Templates shows each template's checks by page (pass, fail, out of date, not run), what failed, the live checks of an unsaved draft and the approvals given despite failing checks.
- **Images** (`supabase/functions/_shared/template-images.ts`, table `site_template_images`, migration 00021): each template's images are **named slots**, one per `{{ASSET:key}}`: hero, services, about, gallery, team, map, product, background or other (`slotOf()`: from what the page's alt text says the image shows, a map or a portrait, else from the key). Each slot records the image's **source** (`architect-ai` as imported; `ai-generated`, `licensed` or `own`), its **licence** (required for a licensed photo; unknown for imported ones until confirmed) or **how it was generated** (model, prompt, date; required for an AI-generated one), the template's **category**, its **intended use** (the page's alt text), its **size** and **SHA-256**, and its **review status** (unreviewed, approved, rejected; an imported or licensed image is approved only once its licence is recorded). FlyttGo administrators only. `report.md` lists the templates that share the very same image set (by SHA-256 where the images were copied, else by address): the screenshot review found 192 of 210 templates using one set of shipping-container photos. **No new photos are sourced yet**: the owner decides between AI-generated, licensed and own photos.
  - **Replacing one slot:** Admin → White label → Templates → a template → Images: approve or reject each image (recording the licence of an imported one), or **Replace…**: the file is checked by its bytes in the browser (PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 5 MB; never by its name or claimed type), uploaded to `site-template-assets/<template>/<key>-<hash>.<ext>` (administrators may upload there; the bucket accepts only those types) and recorded with where it comes from (`admin_replace_site_template_image`; the earlier image is kept in the slot's history). The template goes back to review, and a later re-import keeps the replaced image. The server does not re-read the bytes (the database cannot); a server-side check would be an Edge Function (not built).
  - **Image manifest** (how a later batch plugs in): `node scripts/import-site-templates.ts … --images batch/manifest.json` with
    ```json
    { "version": 1,
      "images": [
        { "template": "salon-verve01", "key": "hero", "file": "verve-hero.webp", "source": "ai-generated",
          "generation": { "model": "<model>", "prompt": "<prompt>", "date": "2026-11-02" },
          "intended_use": "A stylist at work in a bright salon" },
        { "category": "transport", "slot": "hero", "file": "moving-hero-1.jpg", "source": "licensed",
          "licence": "<licence name and reference>" },
        { "category": "transport", "slot": "hero", "file": "moving-hero-2.jpg", "source": "own" }
      ] }
    ```
    An entry names one template's key, or a category and slot: several entries for the same category and slot are shared out in turn, so the templates of a category do not all get the same picture; a template's own entry comes first. Files are relative to the manifest, checked by their bytes (PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 5 MB); every problem is listed before anything is done (`readManifest()`). `--out` copies the images into `<out>/images/<template>/` for review (the thumbnails there still show the earlier images); `--to-flyttgo` uploads them beside the template's images under their hash, records their source and licence or generation, and draws the thumbnail and the browser checks again with them.
  - **Companies' own pictures and the shared library:** see [Image library](#image-library) (00023): a company replaces a slot's picture with its own photo or a library photo, without touching the template; every template draws its photos from the library of its category.
- **Review:** an imported template is not offered. In Admin → White label → Templates (filter by category and by status) an administrator reads each page (sandboxed preview), edits it to remove sample prices, reviews and claims (the database refuses a page with scripts, handlers, frames or forms, and approval while any price remains), confirms the check and approves; a page flagged hidden is fixed first. A re-imported or edited template goes back to review.
- **Serving:** `white-label-site` renders the requested page with the company's brand (`renderTemplatePage`, which sanitises again), and the browser shows it in an iframe **sandboxed without scripts or same-origin access**. Links leave the frame to the site's other pages or the booking page.

## Image library

Owner's decision ([`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) #45; migration 00023, **not yet applied anywhere**): instead of fixed photos per template (almost every imported template points at the same shipping-container set, so a salon's site showed containers), every image slot of every template draws from **one shared image library**. Nothing is in the library yet: it is filled by FlyttGo's administrators and by the library importer, and every photo waits for a person's approval.

- **What a slot shows** (`site_template_image_map(template, organization)`, used by `white-label-site` on the server and by every preview; the renderer takes it through `renderImages()` in `supabase/functions/_shared/image-library.ts`), in this order:
  1. **the company's own image** for that slot (`organization_site_images`): its upload, or a photo it picked from the approved library;
  2. **the template's own image** (`site_template_images`, 00021) when it is approved **and its licence is known** (an imported image still marked approved without a licence counts as not approved);
  3. **an approved library image of the template's category** in the slot's shape, chosen deterministically (below);
  4. **a neutral block** in the company's main colour (an SVG rectangle, no photo; its alt text is empty, it is decoration).

  Never a pending, rejected or retired image, never one whose licence is unknown, never a photo of another category.
- **Backward compatible:** while the template's category has **no approved library image at all**, steps 3 and 4 are replaced by the template's assets exactly as before (`mode: "assets"`). From the category's first approved library image on (`mode: "library"`), the shared imported set is no longer used anywhere in that category; a slot the library cannot fill shows the neutral block. If the map cannot be read (00023 not applied yet), `white-label-site` and the previews render with the assets as before.
- **Whose category:** the **template's** (`site_template_category()` in the database, kept equal to `TEMPLATE_CATEGORIES`; a Vitest test compares the two lists), also when a company in another industry chose a template outside its own: the template's pictures must match its design and wording (a salon template keeps salon photos). A company that wants other photos uses its own images.
- **Shapes:** library images are `hero` (≥ 1.6 : 1 and at least 1200 px wide), `wide` (from 1.2 : 1), `square` (above 0.83 : 1) or `portrait`, computed from the size (`image_shape_of()`, `shapeOf()`) and editable. A slot's shape comes from what it is (its recorded slot, else its key): hero and background → hero; team → portrait; product → square; services, about, gallery → wide; any other slot the shape of the image it had, else wide. A hero slot also takes wide photos and a wide slot hero ones (the slot's own shape first). A **map slot never takes a library photo** (only the company's or the template's own image, else the neutral block).
- **Deterministic choice, no repeats on a page:** slots are filled in the order they first appear in the pages; for each, among the approved images of the category in a shape it takes, the one (1) not yet used on a page the slot is on, (2) least used in the template, (3) of the slot's own shape, (4) first by `md5(template id / slot key / image SHA-256)`. The same template always shows the same photos; templates of a category differ; adding an image to the pool changes only the slots it wins. When the pool is smaller than a page needs, photos repeat, but every slot is still filled.
- **Thumbnails** (owner's decision 2026-10-30, [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) #47; migration 00024, **not yet applied anywhere**): each thumbnail records the images it was drawn with (`images` on the template's `thumbnail` asset: the SHA-256 of the **picture key** of the resolved map without a company, `site_template_images_key()` in the database and `thumbnailImagesKey()` in `_shared/image-library.ts`, which write the same text: one line per slot in key order, `<key>=<address, or bucket/path, or - for the neutral block>|<focal x × 1000>|<focal y × 1000>`; alt texts and sources are left out because they do not change the picture).
  - **Fresh, stale, missing:** a thumbnail is *stale* when the images its template shows now differ from those it was drawn with, *missing* when there is none. This is **computed at each read** from `site_template_image_map()`, never a flag set by hand: approving, rejecting, retiring or editing (shape, focal point) a library photo of the category, or approving, replacing or rejecting the template's own image, makes the affected thumbnails stale at once, and approving a retired photo again makes them fresh again. A thumbnail drawn before 00024 (nothing recorded) counts as drawn with the template's own assets as they are now (`site_template_assets_map()`): it is stale once its category has library photos or one of its slots shows another image; an image replaced in a category without library photos before the thumbnail was ever redrawn is not detected (its asset address was changed with it). The importer now records what it drew with (its assets), and a re-import replaces the record with the assets.
  - **Who reads it:** `admin_site_template_thumbnails(category)` (FlyttGo's administrators and the server: every template not retired, with its state, the images it was drawn with and those it shows now) and `admin_thumbnail_stale_counts()` (per category: templates, fresh, stale, missing). Signed-in users get only the ids of the offered templates whose thumbnail is stale (`stale_site_template_thumbnails()`), for the gallery.
  - **The redraw** (`scripts/redraw-thumbnails.ts`): reads the list with the service key and draws the stale thumbnails and those without one (owner's decision 2026-10-30; `--stale-only` skips the missing ones; `--ids a,b` or `--all` whatever their state; `--category`, `--limit`): for each it reads the stored pages and the resolved map from FlyttGo, renders the home page as the template's own demo (`demoBrand()`: its name and colours, sample contact details) with the resolved images, and takes the picture as the importer does (1440 × 900 window, 720 × 450 JPEG, images only from the hosts the page uses). A **dry run** by default: the pictures go to `thumbnail-redraw/` (`--out`) with `plan.json`, nothing is written to FlyttGo. With `--upload` (and the owner's approval: it writes to FlyttGo's storage and database) each is stored at `site-template-assets/<id>/thumbnail-<first 12 hex of the images>.jpg`, a **new name for new images** so no browser shows the old picture from its cache (the earlier file stays for a few days, so it can be restored), and recorded with `record_site_template_thumbnail()` (server only; the template's review status is unchanged). A picture whose images changed while the list was read is recorded with the images it shows (and is then stale again). The server cannot draw pages (no browser in the database or the Edge Functions): it runs where Playwright and a Chromium are installed (`npm i -D playwright && npx playwright install chromium`), with Node 22.21 or later; behind an HTTPS proxy Node's own `fetch` ignores `HTTPS_PROXY` unless `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1` is set (the browser takes the proxy from `HTTPS_PROXY` by itself):

    ```
    NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 FLYTTGO_SUPABASE_URL=https://<ref>.supabase.co FLYTTGO_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY=… \
      node scripts/redraw-thumbnails.ts [--category appointments] [--stale-only] [--upload]
      node scripts/redraw-thumbnails.ts --clean-old [--older-than 7] [--delete]
    ```
  - **Earlier thumbnail files** (owner's decision 2026-10-30): removed after a few days. `--clean-old` lists, per template, the thumbnail files (`thumbnail.jpg` from the import, `thumbnail-<hex>.jpg` from a redraw) that are not its current one and are older than `--older-than` days (default 7); `--delete` removes them from storage. A template without a current thumbnail keeps all its files, and nothing else in the bucket is touched. Once the library has approved photos for a category, run one full redraw (`--all`) so that templates whose image was replaced before 00024 are drawn again too (they cannot be detected as stale).
  - **The gallery while a thumbnail is stale:** the card shows its home page **live** (`TemplateThumb`) as the template's own demo with the resolved images (the map without a company, loaded once per template), drawn only when the card comes into view; when the home page or the images cannot be loaded it shows the plain card, never the template's imported photos (`cardPicture()` in `gallery.ts`, `thumbSource()`). Fresh thumbnails stay pictures and templates without one stay plain cards. Where the database cannot say (00024 not applied, or before the list has loaded), a thumbnail in a category with library photos counts as stale. The company's current choice in the Website card is drawn live in its own name, colours and images while its thumbnail is stale.
  - **Admin → White label → Image library → Thumbnails:** per category, how many thumbnails are up to date, stale or missing, and the command that draws them again (dry run and `--upload`), with the reason it is a command: the server cannot run a browser.
- **The library** (`image_library`): one row per photo, stored in the public bucket `site-image-library` at `<category>/<sha256>.<ext>` (PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 8 MB, checked by its bytes): category, shape, size, optional focal point (applied as `object-position` on an `<img>` without its own style), tags, alt text, origin (where it comes from), licence, the evidence of the licence, sources, SHA-256 (unique: the same photo is never added twice), review status (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`, `retired`) with reviewer, time and reason, and its whole **history** (who added, edited, approved, rejected or retired it, when, why). Approval needs the licence, its evidence and an alt text (also a constraint on the table); rejecting and retiring need a reason; a changed shape, origin, licence or evidence sends an approved photo back to review; the category is part of the storage path and is not changed in place. Photos are never deleted, only retired. Signed-in users read approved photos only; FlyttGo administrators all.
- **Admin → White label → Image library** (`src/components/admin/ImageLibraryAdmin.tsx`): upload (bytes checked, shape suggested from the size), category, shape, tags, alt text, origin, licence and evidence; filter by category, shape and status; approve, reject, retire, back to pending, edit; the history of each photo. **Coverage by category** (`admin_image_library_coverage()`): per category and shape, the approved (and pending) photos against what the category's templates need: the most slots of that shape in one template (so a page need not repeat a photo; red while short) and the slots in all of them.
- **A company's own images** (Corporate → White label → Website → *Pictures on your website*, `src/components/whiteLabel/SiteImages.tsx`): for the template it saved, each slot with what it shows now and where that comes from; owners and admins **upload their own** (PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 8 MB, checked by its bytes in the browser, stored in the company's own folder of the private bucket `organization-site-images`: `<organization id>/<key>-<hash>.<ext>`), **choose from the library** (approved photos of the template's category in a shape the slot takes), or **reset to default**. `set_organization_site_image()` and `reset_organization_site_image()` check the role (owners and admins of that company only; not other members, not FlyttGo's administrators), that the upload is in the company's folder and that a library pick is approved and of the template's category; each change is in the company's log (`white_label.site_image`). The company's members read its rows; nobody else does: the published site receives only the rendered page, from the server, which signs the company's uploads for an hour (as the logo). A picked library photo retired later is no longer shown. The bytes of an upload are checked in the browser; the bucket restricts type and size; the server does not read the bytes again (as for logos and 00021's replacements).
- **Catalogue** (how a harvested batch plugs in): `node scripts/import-image-library.ts --library <catalogue.json> --library-dir <dir>` is a **dry run** by default: it reads the catalogue, checks every file by its bytes (PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 8 MB; the SHA-256 and the size in pixels must match the catalogue) and lists what can be added, per category and shape, and what is refused and why. With `--upload` (and `FLYTTGO_SUPABASE_URL`, `FLYTTGO_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`; needs the owner's approval, it writes to FlyttGo's storage and database) it stores each new photo at `site-image-library/<category>/<sha256>.<ext>` and records it with `admin_add_library_image()` as **pending**; photos already in the library (same SHA-256) are left as they are. Options `--category`, `--limit`. The catalogue (`readCatalogue()`):

    ```json
    { "version": 1,
      "images": [
        { "sha256": "<64 lower-case hex: SHA-256 of the file's bytes>",
          "file": "appointments/salon-0001.jpg",
          "width": 1920, "height": 1080,
          "category": "appointments",
          "shape": "hero",
          "tags": ["salon", "hair", "interior"],
          "alt": "A stylist cutting a client's hair in a bright salon",
          "origin": "unsplash",
          "licence": "Unsplash Licence",
          "licence_evidence": "https://unsplash.com/license",
          "sources": [{ "url": "https://unsplash.com/photos/<id>", "author": "<name>", "retrieved": "2026-10-30" }],
          "focal_x": 0.5, "focal_y": 0.4 }
      ] }
    ```

    Required: `sha256`, `file` (relative to `--library-dir`, inside it), `width` and `height` (pixels, as the bytes say), `category` (`transport`, `trades`, `appointments`, `professional`, `hospitality`, `retail`, `technology`, `community`, `industry`, `entertainment` or `other`), `origin` (where it comes from, at most 80 characters) and `licence` (an image whose licence is unknown is never added). Optional: `shape` (else computed from the size), `tags`, `alt` (needed before approval), `licence_evidence` (needed before approval), `sources` (addresses or objects, kept as given), `focal_x`/`focal_y` (0–1). A bare list of images is accepted too. Every problem is listed before anything is done; the same image twice (same `sha256`) is refused.
- **AI pilot** (owner's decision 2026-10-30, [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) #50): photos generated with the OpenAI Images API (model `gpt-image-1`; the owner calls it "ChatGPT") by `scripts/generate-library-images.ts`, **once**, reviewed by a person and loaded through the loader above as **pending**; never generated on page load. A small, cost-controlled pilot **per category** (not per template), first the categories without any photos: `trades` (plumbing, electricians, cleaning, car repair, construction) and `appointments` (salons, barbers, beauty, wellness, fitness; never clinics or health), later `transport` (moving, vans, couriers, warehouses); about 10–20 images per category in a mix of shapes.
  - **Prompt plans** (`scripts/image-prompts/<category>.json`, checked in): a style shared by the category (realistic photography of small Norwegian and Nordic businesses, natural light) and subject briefs, each with its shape, tags and alt text (adults of different ages, fictional people, plain unmarked clothes, vehicles and products). Every prompt ends with the same rules (`SAFETY`): no text, letters, numbers, signage, labels, logos, brand names or watermarks; only adults, no children; no one resembling a real or famous person; no medical, clinical or hospital setting. `readPlan()` refuses a brief whose subject asks for any of these, a health tag, a missing alt text or an id used twice. The plans hold more briefs than a pilot takes (trades 26, appointments 25, transport 16, every shape at least 3); a run takes `--limit` (default 12) across the shapes in turn, and with `--coverage <file>` (the rows of `admin_image_library_coverage()` as JSON) the shapes the category is short of first (the most slots of the shape in one template less its approved and pending photos).
  - **Shapes and sizes:** hero and wide are asked for at 1536 × 1024, square at 1024 × 1024, portrait at 1024 × 1536. 1536 × 1024 is 1.5 : 1, which the library counts as *wide*, so a hero brief is cropped to its middle 1536 × 960 (1.6 : 1, a hero) in a local browser (Playwright; without it, or with `--no-crop`, it stays 1536 × 1024 and is recorded as wide, which hero slots also take). The catalogue records the shape from the image's actual size.
  - **What to set:** an OpenAI **API** key from platform.openai.com in `OPENAI_API_KEY` (API use is billed separately from a ChatGPT subscription, which does not include it), and network access to `api.openai.com`. Behind an HTTPS proxy Node's own `fetch` ignores `HTTPS_PROXY` unless `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1` is set (the script warns). The key is sent only in the request's Authorization header and never printed; error messages from the API are printed with keys masked.
  - **Dry run first** (the default; nothing is sent): `node scripts/generate-library-images.ts --category trades [--limit 12]` prints the plan (how many per shape, every prompt and size) and an **estimated cost** from the price table `scripts/image-prompts/prices.json` (or `--prices`, and `--price-per-image 1536x1024=0.08,1024x1024:high=0.2` per size and quality). The table's figures are **assumptions, not OpenAI's prices**: check https://openai.com/api/pricing/ before a run and set them. A run whose estimate is above `--max-cost` (default 5, in the table's currency), or that has a size without a price, is refused without `--yes`; during a run the next image is not generated when it would take the running estimate above `--max-cost`.
  - **Generate:** `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 OPENAI_API_KEY=… node scripts/generate-library-images.ts --category trades --limit 12 --generate` (`--quality low|medium|high`, default medium; `--format jpeg|png|webp`, default jpeg). One request per image (`n = 1`), one after the other, retried on 429 (honouring `Retry-After`) and 5xx with backoff (`--retries`, default 4); any other error stops the run. Into `--out` (default `ai-images/<category>/`, not committed): the images (`<category>-<brief>.<ext>`); `library-catalogue.json`, the loader's catalogue (origin "AI-generated (OpenAI gpt-image-1)", licence "OpenAI output — commercial use per OpenAI terms, owner to confirm", licence evidence https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use, sources with the prompt, model, quality, size, crop, brief and date, alt text from the brief, focal point 0.5/0.5), written after every image so an interrupted run keeps what it made; `cost-log.json`, per image the **usage the API returned** (tokens), the estimate and, when `per_million_tokens` is set in the table, the cost computed from the tokens; and `contact-sheet.html` (with `contact-sheet.png` when Playwright is installed). A brief already in the catalogue is not generated again (`--regenerate`).
  - **Review:** open the contact sheet and check every image for quality (hands, faces, tools, perspective), a misleading depiction (work done unsafely or wrongly, a setting such a company would not have), any accidental text, letters or numbers, logos, brand marks or watermarks, children, medical settings and any likeness of a real person. Take a failing image out before loading: `--drop <brief>[,<brief>] --reason "…"` moves its file to `rejected/`, removes it from the catalogue and records it in `review-log.json`.
  - **Load as pending, then approve:** `node scripts/import-image-library.ts --library ai-images/trades/library-catalogue.json` (dry run), then `--upload` with the owner's approval. The generator itself **never uploads** (`--upload` on it is refused). Each image is approved in Admin → White label → Image library only after the owner has confirmed OpenAI's terms for commercial use on customers' sites ([`RELEASE_GATES.md`](RELEASE_GATES.md) → Website template photos), with the alt text read again. The pure parts (plans, shapes and sizes, the selection, the estimate and the guard, the API call with a mocked answer, the catalogue entry and the contact sheet) are tested in `src/test/ai-images.test.ts`.
- **Rollback:** `supabase/rollback/00023_down.sql` drops the library's records and companies' own images (export them first); the files stay in storage (the two buckets are removed only when empty); sites go back to their templates' images. Tested by `run.sh` (state restored exactly, with data, and in the release rehearsal).

## Booking widget

A company puts its booking page on its own website (migration 00018; the API keys of the same migration are in [`API.md`](API.md)). **No API key is involved:** customers book through the company's FlyttGo page, as on `/b/<slug>`. **Included in every plan** (owner's decision 2026-10-29): any active company with an active plan can use it; API access is a separate add-on ([`API.md`](API.md)).

- **The snippet** (Corporate dashboard → **API and widget**, with a copy button and a live preview):
  ```html
  <div id="flyttgo-booking"></div>
  <script src="https://flyttgo.no/widget.js" data-company="<slug>" data-target="#flyttgo-booking" async></script>
  ```
  `public/widget.js` (no dependencies) inserts an iframe of `/embed/<slug>` and sets its height from the page's `flyttgo:resize` messages, accepted only from its own frame and FlyttGo's origin. The frame is sandboxed: scripts, forms and new tabs for FlyttGo's page; it never navigates the company's page without the visitor's click.
- **The embed page** (`src/pages/EmbedPage.tsx`) shows the same booking flow as the company's page, compact and without FlyttGo's header and footer: FlyttGo's moves (`BookingFlow`) for transport plans, the company's jobs or appointments (`ServiceBookingPage`) for the others. It needs the company's page to be active. Card and Vipps payment open in a new tab (payment providers refuse to run inside frames; the confirmation page there may ask the customer to sign in again, as browsers keep a frame's sign-in apart from the site's); sign-in works inside the frame with email and password (Google or Apple sign-in may not, as they refuse frames).
- **Where it may be shown:** the websites the company lists (owners and admins; `https://host[:port]` without a path, or `http://localhost` for testing; at most 20), in `organization_widget_origins`. Enforced twice:
  1. **The browser**, by `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'self' <the websites>` on `/embed/<slug>`. `vercel.json` only rewrites to the app, so the header comes from the Vercel Routing Middleware `middleware.ts`, which reads the list through `white-label-site` (`"widget": true`). If the list cannot be read only FlyttGo may frame the page.
  2. **The embed page**, which checks the website that framed it (`location.ancestorOrigins`, else the referrer, else the origin the script passed) against the list from `white-label-site` and shows the flow only there or inside FlyttGo itself (the dashboard's preview). Elsewhere it shows a link to the company's page. This also covers hosts where the middleware does not run (local development).
- **Before going live:** check on a Vercel preview that `/embed/<slug>` carries the header (the middleware is not tested on Vercel yet; [`DEPLOYMENT.md`](DEPLOYMENT.md) §3).

## Before going live: checklist

1. Confirm or change the prices (Admin → White label → Plans and prices).
2. **Legal:** have a lawyer review the data processing agreement (it is marked as a draft) and the Model B terms (subcontracting, liability, consumer rights split), and the receipts each model issues.
3. **Accountant:** the plan invoices and the Model B settlement lines (transport resale and platform fee, MVA).
4. **Stripe Connect** on FlyttGo's Stripe account (Standard accounts), a Connect webhook endpoint to `stripe-webhook` with `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `refund.*` and `account.updated`, and the secret `STRIPE_CONNECT_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.
5. **Hosting:** a wildcard domain `*.flyttgo.no` on the Vercel project for subdomains; each verified company domain added to the project (Vercel → Domains). `WHITE_LABEL_CNAME_TARGET` if the target is not `cname.vercel-dns.com`.
6. Settings `white_label_support_email` / `white_label_support_phone` (Model A pages' customer service; otherwise the invoice seller's).
7. Import and review the templates (above).
